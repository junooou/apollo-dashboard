# Voncierge LinkedIn Connection Note Guidelines

Reference for generating LinkedIn connection request notes sent from Nikhil
Gupta's own LinkedIn account — he is the CEO of Voncierge, and these notes go
out under his name, not Amanda's (who owns the email sequences in
`voncierge_outreach_generation_guidelines.md`). This is a different
discipline from those emails: a connection note is capped at 300 characters.
That's enough room for two short sentences, not just one — use the space:
a specific, personalized reason you're reaching out to *this* person, plus
who Nikhil is and why he's reaching out. A note that stops at 130-150
characters is leaving real, useful space on the table — treat 220-280 as the
actual target, not 300 as a ceiling you're avoiding.

Each note is generated for one specific, already-known contact (real first
name, title, seniority, location, company), not a reusable template — do not
use merge fields like `{firstname}` here.

## Available personalization inputs

Per contact, you may be given: `firstname`, `title`, `seniority`, `location`,
`company`. At the company level, you may also be given a **detected signal**
— a matching News Trigger or Job Signal already scored elsewhere in this
tool (e.g. a funding round, a store opening, a relevant open role) — when one
clears that tool's own relevance bar. Not every contact or company will have
all of these; use only what's actually provided.

- Treat a detected signal as **optional seasoning, not a requirement.** It's
  a company-level fact, so if you're drafting for several contacts at the
  same company, do not paste the same signal-based sentence into every note —
  use it for at most one or two contacts where it fits naturally, and lean on
  title/seniority/location for the rest.
- When drafting **multiple contacts at the same company in one batch**, each
  note must read as individually written for that person: vary the opening
  hook, sentence structure, and which detail (title vs. seniority vs.
  location vs. the detected signal) anchors the personalization. Two notes
  that would read near-identically with just the name swapped is a failure
  — rewrite one of them.

## Constraints

- Hard cap: 300 characters, including spaces. **Target 220-280** — this is
  the actual goal, not an upper bound to stay safely under. A note under 180
  characters is under-using the space and should be expanded, not treated as
  "done."
- Open with "Hi {their first name}," then get straight to the point.
- Reference the recipient's actual title, seniority, or location naturally,
  using only what's provided — never invent details about them. With 300
  characters available, use at least one specific, concrete detail beyond
  just their name (their exact title, a detected signal, their city) — a
  note that could be sent to anyone at the company unchanged is too generic
  for the space it has.
- **Four parts, always, in this order — budget space for all four rather
  than spending the whole note on the first two:**
  1. **Greeting** (~10 chars): "Hi {firstname},"
  2. **Personalized hook** (~90-110 chars): a specific, concrete reason
     Nikhil is reaching out to *this* person — their role, a detected
     signal, their location — not generic.
  3. **What Voncierge does, for them specifically** (~90-110 chars): who
     Nikhil is (CEO/founder) and what Voncierge does, framed toward
     something relevant to *this* recipient's world (their industry, their
     function) rather than a generic capability list.
  4. **Closing** (~15-30 chars): a short, warm connect-oriented line —
     "Would love to connect.", "Keen to connect.", "Let's connect." A note
     that ends on a company fact or capability instead of a closing line is
     incomplete, even if it's under the character cap.
  No hard CTA inside the note itself ("would you be open to a call") — a
  connection request that asks for something beyond connecting converts
  worse than one that just states the reason and closes warmly. The ask for
  a call can wait for the first real message after connecting.
- **Never let part 2 or 3 run long enough to crowd out part 4.** If the
  personalized hook and the Voncierge description together leave no room for
  a closing line within 300 characters, trim the hook or the description —
  the closing line is not optional and must never be the first thing cut.
- Only claim what's true of Voncierge's approved positioning: an AI video
  concierge platform for physical spaces (kiosks, malls, airports, offices),
  human-escalation-first, live in production at Changi Airport, Jewel,
  Cushman & Wakefield, and Workforce Singapore/e2i.
- Never imply Voncierge replaces staff.
- Identify Nikhil as CEO/founder of Voncierge, not "growth" or "sales" —
  he's messaging from his own account, so the note should read as a founder
  reaching out, not a rep prospecting. "I'm the CEO of Voncierge" or
  "I founded Voncierge" both work.
- Sign off with "Nikhil" only if space allows after everything else — a bare
  first name is enough; don't add "CEO of Voncierge" again in the sign-off
  if the opening line already said it and space is tight.

## Good examples (structure and length, not to copy verbatim)

These are the actual target length (220-280 characters) and all four parts
are present — greeting, personalized hook, Voncierge value for this
recipient, closing:

- "Hi Wei Ling, [greeting] saw you lead Consumer Banking and Wealth
  Management at DBS — a role that sits right at the intersection of digital
  service and customer experience. [hook] I'm Nikhil, CEO of Voncierge, we
  build AI video concierge for physical spaces, live at Changi Airport.
  [value] Would love to connect. [closing]" — written without the bracket
  labels: "Hi Wei Ling, saw you lead Consumer Banking and Wealth Management
  at DBS — a role that sits right at the intersection of digital service and
  customer experience. I'm Nikhil, CEO of Voncierge, we build AI video
  concierge for physical spaces, live at Changi Airport. Would love to
  connect." (286 characters)
- "Hi Marcus, noticed your CX role at DBS in Singapore — I founded Voncierge,
  an AI video concierge platform for physical spaces like airports and
  malls, currently live at Changi and Jewel. Keen to connect and hear how
  your team is approaching digital service today." (263 characters, closing
  line folded into a specific forward-looking ask rather than a bare "keen
  to connect")
- "Hi Priya, saw Royal Caribbean's new year-round Singapore season for
  Navigator of the Seas — exciting expansion. I'm Nikhil, CEO of Voncierge,
  we build AI video concierge for multilingual passenger support, live at
  Changi. Would be great to connect." (248 characters, using a detected
  signal as the personalization anchor)

These are failures, even though both are under the 300-character cap:

- "Hi Wei Ling, I'm CEO of Voncierge — we build AI video concierge for
  airports and malls, live at Changi. Would love to connect." (130
  characters — generic, doesn't use the recipient's actual role, wastes more
  than half the available space)
- "Hi Tse, your leadership of Consumer Banking and Wealth Management at DBS
  sits at the intersection of customer service and digital experience. I'm
  Nikhil, CEO of Voncierge; we build human-escalation-first AI video
  concierge for physical spaces, live at Changi Airport." (267 characters —
  good length and a real personalized hook, but it stops after the company
  description with no closing line at all. This is the most common failure
  mode: don't let the hook and description eat the entire budget.)

## Bad patterns to avoid

- Anything that reads as mass-sent ("Hi there," "Dear Sir/Madam").
- A hard CTA inside the note ("would you be open to a 15-min call").
- Buzzword-heavy openers ("Hope this finds you well!").
- Exceeding 300 characters — LinkedIn truncates or blocks the send.
- Stopping at 130-180 characters "to be safe." That's under-using the space,
  not being concise — it usually means the note skipped the recipient's
  specific detail entirely. Every draft should be checked against the
  220-280 target before being returned, not just against the 300 ceiling.
- Ending on a capability/company fact instead of a closing line (e.g. "...
  live at Changi Airport." with nothing after it). Every draft must end with
  one of the connect-oriented closing phrases from part 4 above — check the
  last few words of every draft before returning it.
